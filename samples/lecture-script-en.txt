Today we're going to talk about gradient descent.
The model moves its weights a little at a time in the direction that lowers the loss function.
How far it moves in one step is set by the learning rate.
If the learning rate is too large, the update overshoots the minimum and the loss can actually diverge.
If it is too small, training becomes very slow and may not converge within the epochs you have.
Instead of using the whole dataset at once, we can compute the gradient on a small batch of examples. This is called stochastic gradient descent.
With mini-batches each update is fast, and the small amount of noise in the gradient can even help the model escape shallow local minima.
In practice, people often start with a large learning rate and shrink it over time. This is called learning rate scheduling.
Two common schedules are step decay, which halves the learning rate every few epochs, and cosine annealing, which lowers it along a cosine curve.
